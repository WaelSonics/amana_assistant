## Why a v5
After the breaker the client asked for a no-stop, long-term part next to the trading part (10/23). Removing the AMD stop was
rejected because it is a conversion of a losing trade. The idea itself is sound if it is built in advance, with rules. v4
(VOO 80%) is one answer. v5 is the middle: VOO plus a small set of quality compounders held without price stops, plus a
smaller trading pot under v3's rules.

## v5 rules (proposal)
| Bucket | Target | Holds | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | **50%** | VOO | Never sold, no stop. The $200 a month goes here unless a compounder is > 2 points under its target |
| **Compounders** | **30%** | 6 quality names, one per sector, ≈ 5% each at cost, max 1 AI-platform name | See below |
| **Trading pot** | **15%** | ≤ 3 names under the v3 rules (stop written first, R/R ≥ 2:1, stop ≥ 1.5 ATR, chase guard, ratchet), AI semis ≤ 2 | Pot breaker: −25% from the pot's high → halve the pot. Override #1 → pot cut to 7.5%; #2 → pot closed into VOO |
| **Reserve** | **5%** | Cash | VOO index ladder only (−7% / −15%) |

**Compounder rules:**
1. **No price stop.** Exits are written thesis breaks per name (e.g. two guidance cuts in a row, a regulatory ruling that cuts
   the core economics, a dividend cut), reviewed after each report.
2. **Bought on fixed dates**, in tranches on the first trading day of January and February, with no timing. **Valuation gate:**
   no buy above 35× forward earnings. A name above the gate waits, and its slice goes to VOO.
3. **−40% from cost** with the thesis intact → a written review. Not a sale, and no add until the next report.
4. **Fresh buys only.** Never a conversion of a trading position. No name enters within 30 days of leaving the trading pot.
5. Trim only above 10% of the sleeve. The list changes only at the quarterly review (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct), at most one swap a
   quarter, decided with the desk on a weekend.

**What v5 removes:** the account-level −10% circuit breaker. Core and compounders are meant to ride drawdowns. **The price:
the backtest's worst drop is −44% (2008). If the client would sell at −30%, v5 is the wrong version.**

## Backtest (rules followed, $3,300 + $200/month, $1 a fill, Yahoo monthly adjusted closes, no stops on compounders)
- v5 is modelled as 70% S&P (VOO 50% + reserve 5% + trading 15%) and 30% compounders. The trading pot is modelled as the S&P.
  The 10/18 study found perfectly followed v3 trading ≈ the index, and at the real fill pace ≈ 10% worse, which would cost
  v5 ≈ −1.5% of final value.
- "Worst drop" is time-weighted (deposits excluded).

| Window | S&P | **v5, final list** (hindsight) | **v5, 2006 top-5 list** (no hindsight: XOM, GE, MSFT, C, BAC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 y (2006-10 → 2026-10, $51,300 in) | $242,083 · −51% | $296,554–306,232 (**+$54–64k**) · −44% | $253,453–254,632 (**+$11–13k**) · −56 to −58% |
| 10 y ($27,300 in) | $62,972 · −24% | +$5.1–5.5k · −20% | +$8.4–10.0k · −23% |
| 5 y ($15,300 in) | $22,519 · −24% | +$1.3–1.5k · −20% | +$3.8–3.9k · −21% |

The ranges come from two deposit rules: most-underweight name vs fixed rotation.

**The 10/18 study for comparison** (its own simulation, S&P $251,182 over 20 y):
- v3: −$11k to −$21k vs the S&P.
- v4: −$3.5k to +$3k.
- Worst drops: v3 −46/−47%, v4 −49/−52%.

**How to read it:**
1. **The final-list result is hindsight.** These names were picked because they did well. That number is not evidence.
2. **The only fair test is the 2006 top-5.** Citigroup lost 97%, BAC 92% and GE 79%, yet 30% in that list still beat the
   S&P by $11–13k over 20 years. Most of that came from buying the fallen names cheaply in 2009 with each month's money.
   The cost was a deeper worst drop (−58% vs −51%). It is one list, one draw.
3. **Random 5-name lists from today's 54 large caps beat the S&P 294 of 300 times. That is survivorship bias, not
   skill**: the universe contains only companies that survived and grew. Bessembinder (2018): ≈ 57% of US stocks
   returned less than T-bills over their lifetime.
4. **Bottom line:** perfectly followed, v5 looks like the S&P plus a modest, uncertain quality premium, with similar or
   deeper crashes. v4 is the S&P. v3 trailed. **The deciding variable is behaviour, not the rules.**
